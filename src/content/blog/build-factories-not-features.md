Two months ago, I was laid off.

There's no clever framing that makes that land softer. One day I had a team and a roadmap; the next I had a severance email and a lot of quiet mornings.

What I didn't expect was how much those mornings would change the question I was asking. At first it was obvious: how do I get the next job fast? But it kept shifting into something harder: what does being an engineer even mean from here? The job I lost isn't quite the job I'm applying for anymore.

So I did what a lot of us do when we're scared with time on our hands. I read and watched too much. But one talk, Andrej Karpathy's "From Vibe Coding to Agentic Engineering" at Sequoia's AI Ascent, changed how I saw the question.

One line stuck:

> You can outsource your thinking, but you can never outsource your understanding.

It's easy to point an agent at a problem, get something that runs, and feel productive. Karpathy calls that "vibe coding", and it's seductive, especially when you're anxious and want to feel capable again. He contrasts it with agentic engineering: designing the system that builds the system. Give two engineers the same agent and the same context, and they get wildly different output. The difference is their judgment.

The layoff made me question whether my judgment was worth much anymore. It's tempting to think the AI is the threat. But what it does is expose the difference between the engineer who understands and the one who was just shipping vibes.

I think agentic engineering is my future, so the question sharpened into something I can act on. How do I become an agentic engineer instead of a vibe coding one?

The idea that has stuck with me is to build factories, not features.

The vibe coder ships the feature. The agentic engineer builds the system of agents and code that ships it for them, a "dark factory." Plan, build, validate, review, release, all templated and reproducible, so one good prompt returns an on-spec result instead of a lucky one. You stop being the worker on the line and become the person who designs the line.

But a factory you don't understand is a liability. Use the agents to build the system, but never ship a line I couldn't explain. The moment I can't, it's just vibe coding at scale. So the work now is building the system and keeping the understanding that makes it trustworthy.

I'm writing this partly to convince myself. But if you're between roles and quietly panicking that the ground is shifting, getting laid off didn't put you behind. We're all a little behind. The window where this is new is closing fast, but there's still time to be early.

If you're becoming an agentic engineer too, or figuring out how, I'd love to hear how you're approaching it.
